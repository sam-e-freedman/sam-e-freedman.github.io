M.S. Geographic Information Science · San Francisco State · 2024–2026

Post-fire ecosystem recovery & forest carbon mapping

My thesis compares two very different California wildfires: the 2021 Dixie Fire in Sierra Nevada mixed conifer and the 2020 CZU Lightning Complex in the coastal redwoods. I quantify how much carbon each forest lost, how it is recovering, and which landscape factors drive burn severity.

  • Python
  • R
  • ArcGIS Pro
  • Remote sensing
  • Machine learning
  • NASA GEDI lidar
  • Sentinel-2 / Landsat
  • Random Forest

Mapping, modeling & machine learning

I convert NASA GEDI lidar measurements of canopy structure into aboveground biomass and carbon (Mg C/ha). Then I use Random Forest models to predict burn severity and produce wall-to-wall carbon maps from terrain, vegetation, and access predictors. Spatial cross-validation keeps the models honest about spatial autocorrelation.

Imagery comes straight from Microsoft Planetary Computer's STAC catalog. Python scripts build yearly cloud-filtered Sentinel-2 and Landsat composites and compute indices such as NDVI and NDMI.

What the two fires show

In the CZU redwoods, burn severity dropped in stands with the most pre-fire carbon. Denser, moister redwood forest seems to have buffered the fire. In the Dixie Fire, carbon density had little effect on severity.

I'm now comparing recovery trajectories by years since fire, burn-severity class, and terrain, with an eye to where post-fire recovery could support carbon and biodiversity credits.

Rock Meadow: a conservation master plan

Belmont Conservation Commission · Belmont, MA

An eco-historical walking tour, an exotic-species management plan, and design alternatives for a meadow and woodland conservation area. A follow-up commission from the Town of Belmont redesigned the flooding parking lot with bioswales and pollinator planting.

Ecological resilience in Deerfield, MA

Franklin Regional Council of Governments

A resilience assessment at the forest, river, and town scales. It mapped floodplain forests and found gaps in riparian buffers. It then combined community priorities with Mass Audubon's MAPPR model to identify three critical restoration areas along the Deerfield River.
