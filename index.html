M.S. Geographic Information Science · San Francisco State · 2024–2026

Post-fire ecosystem recovery & forest carbon mapping

My thesis compared two very different California wildfires: the 2021 Dixie Fire in Sierra Nevada mixed-conifers and the 2020 CZU Lightning Complex in the coastal redwoods. I quantified how much carbon each forest lost, how it recovered, and which landscape factors drove burn severity.

  • Python
  • R
  • ArcGIS Pro
  • Remote sensing
  • Machine learning
  • NASA GEDI lidar
  • Sentinel-2 / Landsat
  • Random Forest

Mapping, modeling & machine learning

I converted NASA GEDI lidar measurements of canopy structure into aboveground biomass and carbon (Mg C/ha). Then I used Random Forest models to predict burn severity and produce wall-to-wall carbon maps from terrain, vegetation, and access predictors. Spatial cross-validation kept the models honest about spatial autocorrelation.

Imagery came straight from Microsoft Planetary Computer's STAC catalog. Python scripts built yearly cloud-filtered Sentinel-2 and Landsat composites and computed indices such as NDVI and NDMI.

What the two fires show

In the CZU redwoods, burn severity dropped in stands with the most pre-fire carbon. Denser, moister redwood forest appears to have buffered the fire. In the Dixie Fire, carbon density had little effect on severity.

I also compared recovery trajectories by years since fire, burn-severity class, and terrain, with an eye to where post-fire recovery could support carbon and biodiversity credits.

03

Master of Forestry · Oregon State University · 2019–2021

Modeling carbon payments & the economics of silviculture and agroforestry

My Master of Forestry final report asked whether agroforestry can compete with conventional timber and grazing on a small New England woodlot, and what happens to each option once landowners are paid for building carbon and charged for removing it. The study site was 42.5 acres of steep, northeast-facing northern hardwoods in southern Vermont.

  • Forest Vegetation Simulator (FVS)
  • Discounted cash flow analysis
  • NPV · LEV · sensitivity analysis
  • Carbon accounting
  • Silviculture
  • Silvopasture & forest farming

Ten scenarios, one landscape

I built discounted cash flow models for ten alternatives, each over its own rotation: maple sugaring, forest farming of American ginseng and shiitake, a 6-megawatt solar array, white pine shelterwood, black walnut and black locust plantations, clearcut-and-graze beef cattle, and three silvopasture systems.

I compared each one on NPV, LEV, and carbon storage, then stress-tested them with sensitivity analyses on discount rate and carbon price.

What the numbers showed

Heavy early harvests carry a steep carbon cost. At $20 per tonne CO₂e, every scenario that began with substantial cutting was unprofitable. For silvopasture, that means planting trees into existing pasture rather than clearing forest.

Diversified, frequent revenue wins. Black locust, with its regular thinnings for timber and fence posts, was the most profitable timber scenario. Forest farming ginseng and shiitake earned about $50,000 per acre by year six on a modest upfront investment.

The Kearns residence

Residential landscape design · Wendell, MA

A residential-scale design grounded in regional ecology: EPA ecoregions, forest cover, and BioMap habitat cores, followed by slope, drainage, and solar envelope analysis of the site. It produced three alternatives: "A Mast Year," "Pond & Pollinators," and "Open & Closed."

Ecological resilience in Deerfield, MA

Franklin Regional Council of Governments

A report for FRCOG, built on tree inventories, that assesses Deerfield's ecological resilience at the forest, river, and town scales. It maps floodplain forests and gaps in riparian buffers. Then it overlays A Conservation Vision with Mass Audubon's MAPPR model to identify three critical restoration areas along the Deerfield River.

Rock Meadow: a conservation master plan

Belmont Conservation Commission · Belmont, MA

Commissioned by the Belmont Conservation Commission: an eco-historical walking tour, an exotic-species management plan, and a parking lot redesign to reduce flooding. The plan sets the site in its regional context, from surficial geology and watersheds to the urban edge, with a transect across its woodlands, meadows, and wetlands.
